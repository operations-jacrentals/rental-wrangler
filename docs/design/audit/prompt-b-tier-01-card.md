## 1 · The surface

The **Units card** — one card from a heavy-equipment rental yard's ops app. It is a dense list
of equipment grouped by lifecycle stage, meant to be read at a glance by someone standing in a
yard, on a phone or a grubby desktop.

**It is a design prototype, not the shipped app.** It runs on demo data and several things are
deliberately inert (§5). Judge the *design*, not the completeness of the wiring.

### Getting it running

The card is a self-contained static folder — no network access needed, nothing to install.

```
docs/design/tier-01-card/
```

Serve that folder with any static server and open the root. For example:

```bash
cd docs/design/tier-01-card && python3 -m http.server 8137
# then open http://localhost:8137/
```

Everything (fonts, textures, runtime) is local. If anything tries to reach the network, that
itself is a finding — report it.

**Drive it at ~380–440px wide.** That is the card's real column width in the app. Check a
narrow width too, but judge it at its design width first.

### The dev switch panel

A small switch panel (`#m1-sw`) rides at the bottom-left. It is a **design-time harness, not
product UI** — out of scope as a surface. You need it only if you want to see the alternates:

| Switch | Positions | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | `current` · `strict` · `bold` | How much of the group's state hue mixes into steel (0% = strict). `current` is the default. |
| **B** | `digital` · `steel` · `hybrid` | A dead switch — see §6.2. Leave it on `digital`. |
| **C** | `on` · `off` | Steel hover glisten. |
| **skin** | `steel` · `halo` | Two finishes. **Audit `steel`**; `halo` is a separate skin and out of scope this run. |

## 2 · Your persona

**Merle**, the yard/shop hand.

Merle has worked the yard for eleven years and knows the machines cold — he can tell you which
excavator burns oil by the sound of it. He has no patience for screens. He checks the card
between jobs with one glove off, usually standing, often when someone is already shouting for
him. He wants to know **what's broken, what's promised to somebody, and what he can hand over
right now** — and he wants it without reading. If the screen doesn't tell him in about two
seconds, he'll go ask someone instead, which is exactly the failure we're hunting.

Keep Prompt A's constant traits: lazy, not sharp, clock-watching, distracted by movement.
Write your findings in his voice.

Merle is **not** the dispatcher, and not office staff. He does not care about billing, invoices,
or customer names except as "who's waiting on this." The card has a staff/mechanic ordering and
an office ordering; you are seeing the **staff/mechanic** one.

## 3 · Dimensions to cover

Run all six of Prompt A's lenses, with two re-aimed:

- **task-clarity**, **emergency-triage**, **correctness**, **glitch-consistency** — as written.
- **wayfinding** — re-aimed. This is *one card in isolation*; there is nowhere to navigate to,
  so do not report the absence of destinations as defects. Ask instead: does the card make
  clear **what would happen** if Merle acted, and does it ever strand him with no way back
  from a state he entered (a filter, an open group, an open row, a search)?
- **notifications-comms-team** — re-aimed. There is no bell, no inbox, no team presence in a
  single card. Do not report those as missing. Ask instead: **what should this card hand off,
  and to whom** — what does Merle learn here that somebody else needs to know, that the card
  gives him no way to pass on?

## 4 · Deliberate — do NOT report these as defects

These look wrong and are not. Each is a locked decision, current as of a full read of
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-20-decisions-ledger.md` through **#212**.

### 4.1 · Materials — the law the whole card is built on

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Static things are steel; changing/interactive things are digital and live in glass.** Names, facts and slot ticks are steel. The dark scanlined "message boards" are glass — and they are the card's only interactive surfaces at each level | Jac's ruling: *"The slots are Steel Elements. The buttons/gates are digital so they live in the message glass. Facts and Names are also Steel elements because they don't change."* Every finish/hover/press rule keys off the **family**, never the element, so a control can't go stale when the language moves | #211, #188 |
| **Steel never glows, ever. Light is emitted by glass only** | That is how the card stays "matte" while still reading as an instrument | #146 |
| **Steel text carries its own panel's gradient, brush and tone — it is not a flat fill** | Law 2: text follows the material by *construction* (`background-clip:text` re-using the panel's recipe), so text and panel cannot drift apart | #189 |
| **Almost nothing is rounded.** Square corners throughout; 45° cut chamfers instead of radii | The identity is machined steel, not plastic. Radii were removed on purpose | #140 |
| **One exception: the pill-shaped action button** | Pill = "this is an action you fire" — the one shape allowed to stay round, and it predates this design | #15, #153 |
| **Every control is mono, not the Archivo body font** | One instrument voice inside the machine; Archivo survives only in prose | #142 |

### 4.2 · The two-level physics — a group and a row behave nothing alike

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **The group HEAD moves; it never lights up.** Opening a group shifts it ~1.5px with a moved shadow — mechanical motion, not emission | Groups are steel **housings**: they open by *moving*. Light is reserved for the level below | #168, #146 |
| **A ROW instead lights up like a powered-on terminal** — a bright coloured laser frame wraps it and a drawer drops | Rows are glass **cartridges**: they open by *lighting*. Two identical mechanisms would collapse the level distinction, so the two deliberately share nothing | #168 |
| **The laser frame is a ROW event and draws its own dark outer ring on all four sides** | Earlier the "frame" was incidental contrast from whatever sat underneath, which is why it read on two sides and vanished on the bottom. It now owns the pairing | #200 (kills #172), #169 |
| **The CRT flicker plays only when the drawer is created — not when a group expands, and not on the row's face** | Group expansion mounts rows without opening drawers, so the animation belongs to the drawer alone | #201 |
| **The drawer is the ONLY hue surface on an open row** — its circuit texture takes the row's tone over a neutral near-black; the head stays neutral | One hue channel per level. Mixing the tone into the navy glass came out muddy — the hue must state itself | #199 |

### 4.3 · The boards are the controls (this is the card's newest idea — see §7)

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Each row has exactly ONE outlined state element** — a dark glass board with a thin tone ring, notched corners and scanlines, showing the state word. There is **no separate status chip** | The chip was **merged into the board**. One element, one job: it shows the state *and* takes the press | #208 |
| **A board shows the present condition at rest and swaps to an action verb on hover** (`> OPEN LOG` on rows, `> SEARCH` on heads) | This is how "Present Condition vs Next Action" is resolved on one surface instead of two competing elements. The swap is opacity-only, so the marquee measurement underneath is untouched | #211 |
| **Clicking a row's board opens the row — and so does clicking the row body** | Jac's explicit call: the board is the labelled affordance, but the whole row stays a target | #211 |
| **Pressing a board does not move.** It dips in brightness and flashes a scanline instead | Steel compresses (law 6); glass *lights*. A travel on glass would blur the material line | #211 |
| **Clicking a group head's board turns it into a search field** that live-filters that group's rows; Esc restores the state word. On a collapsed group it opens the group AND focuses the field in one motion | The card's original head board already had this wiring; it is rehosted in the new board rather than rebuilt | #211 |
| **Boards marquee when their text is too long**, at the footer terminal's own speed, with scanlines and a fixed width derived from the card width (114px at 380px) | The board is the footer terminal's recipe, not a simplified cousin | #185 |
| **The boards line up in a column within each level, but the head column and the row column are not the same column** | Fixed for heads deliberately; sharing one column across both levels needs a wider card or a reversal of which element takes the residual width — both are open calls, not side effects | #196 |
| **The state word on a row's board is the item's biggest lever, expressed as a condition — not a verb** | A verb in the row's status hue would be a Door wearing a Signal's colour, the one combination the vocabulary forbids; and a verb discards the condition Merle needs to judge it. Verbs live in the jump band, not on the gate | #182, #183 |

### 4.4 · The head

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **The head is ONE etched-steel panel.** Its elements sit directly on the steel — there is no large glass "screen" behind them. Glass appears only *inside* the outlined board | Four states were tried in one day; this is where it landed | #198 |
| **The group name sits on a rectangular near-black glass plate in a raised steel bezel**, with a small tone tick embedded in the bezel's right edge | Rebuilt directly from Jac's mockup pixels. The earlier "well" treatments (a chamfered milled pocket) are **superseded** — if you were expecting one, that is stale | #202 (supersedes #193, #187's well) |
| **The head's right edge is chamfered 45° at top-right and bottom-right, with a tiny neutral stud on the midline** | Reads as "a chain of groups continuing down a rail." The stud is inert hardware, deliberately *not* accent orange | #206 |
| **A thin tone-coloured ring traces the head's own chamfered silhouette**, and closed rows wear a much quieter version of the same ring | Frames at every level; the closed-row ring is at 40% mix with no glow, deliberately far quieter than the lit row's laser | #209 |
| **The head's board is large — roughly 40–46% of the head's width** | Sized by reserving space for the rack rather than as a percentage, because a percentage resolves against the wrong container | #210 |

### 4.5 · Slots, counts and the row line

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Slot ticks are chunky — 12×24px, skewed, the same size everywhere** | Jac ruled them "as tall as the message board," uniform over count: *"12px everywhere, fewer fit."* The `+N` counter absorbs the overflow | #212 |
| **`+N` reads to the LEFT of the slots, not the right** | It still counts from the tail — the ticks hidden are the rightmost — it just reads before them | #194 |
| **A head rack often shows only a few ticks and a `+N`** | Direct consequence of the fatter ticks above. Not a fitting bug — `fitRack` recomputes from `offsetWidth` and carries a `ResizeObserver` | #212, #195 |
| **The row reads left-to-right: hex icon · name · dim mono facts · slots · board.** The name is **left**-aligned and truncates with an ellipsis | Supersedes the earlier right-aligned grammar at both levels | #207 (supersedes #176) |
| **The name truncates fairly aggressively (e.g. "Excavat…") so the slot rack keeps its ticks** | The mockup encodes this priority: slots over full names | #207 |
| **NO floating count pins anywhere** — not on the WORK/DONE toggles, not on the group name plates. They exist in the DOM but are hidden | The pins broke physical sense once slots became steel. Jac: *"It's a great feature but now doesn't make physical sense. Hide them for now."* Group counts remain readable in the head board ("6 OPEN") | #204 |

### 4.6 · Colour, hover and press

| What you'll see | Why it is deliberate | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Orange appears only on controls you can turn and on live terminal text** | Orange means *you did this / you can do this*; status colour means *the world is like this*. Blurring them would let a selected row compete with an on-fire row | #137 |
| **Glass carries two colours: readouts cyan, controls orange** — while steel keeps the single-accent rule | Deliberate split for glass only. Note the merged state boards then took their **own tone hue** for ring and text, so cyan now lives mainly in the drawer, the lit row's terminal lines and the footer rail | #197, refined by #209/#210 |
| **Red and yellow chips are filled; blue, green and grey are dark with a thin coloured ring** | Heat must stay the loudest thing on screen; filled cool chips read as glossy candy against steel | labs-decisions (cool-tone key finish) — no dedicated row |
| **Green appears on a row, never as its own group** | Green means "done today" and ages to grey tomorrow — a property of a record, not a bucket | #29 |
| **Nothing lifts or brightens on hover** | Both the `translateY` lift and the brightness highlight were removed on Jac's explicit instruction. Hovering a row name used to lift the name | #186 (supersedes #157's lift clause) |
| **But hover is NOT silent** — steel *glistens* (a neutral, slowed version of the row's own wrap animation), the innermost hovered steel element glistens rather than its container, and a glass board swaps to its action verb | Steel glistens; it never lights up. Neutral, not the state hue, because the hue frame already means "powered on" | #190, #211 |
| **Toggles share one select highlight, with a centered bump sitting fully inside the bottom border** | Unified deliberately; the bump is a separate element from the ring | #205 |
| **No description line under the card title** | Removed; its job moved to the footer terminal and the per-group message boards | #149 |
| **Two console warnings** about `<path> attribute d: "{{ p.icon }}"` and `"{{ c.icon }}"` | Documented in `docs/design/tier-01-card/README.md` as an artefact of unpacking the prototype for serving; rendered output is unaffected. Ignore these two — but **do** report any *other* console error | README |

## 5 · Stubs and demo data — do NOT report as broken

- **All record data is fake.** Machine names, serial numbers, dates and counts are illustrative.
  Do not audit the *values*. **Do** audit whether a number is *presented* misleadingly.
- **Clicking a status element or a board name** can flash `Teleport → …` on the footer terminal
  and go nowhere. The destination is unbuilt on purpose.
- **The expanded-row drawer's terminal content** is illustrative lines, not a real detail view —
  Tier 1 (the full record detail) is still parked behind it. Judge whether the terminal-line
  *idea* communicates well, not whether the specific data is complete.
- **Per-board rollups are placeholders** — Tier 2 owns the real numbers.
- **The globe toggle** in the search well switches its own state and tooltip only.
- **The `#m1-sw` switch panel** is a design-time dials harness (see §1). Out of scope.

## 6 · Known-open — already logged, don't re-litigate

Report these only if you find a consequence we have *not* already named.

1. **Which field is a card's "biggest lever" is deliberately unruled** (#184). The *rule* is
   locked (the gate shows the biggest lever, as state, narrated by the board); *which* field
   that is per card is product content, deferred on purpose. Do not report the specific state
   words as wrong choices — report only if the state shown leaves Merle unable to act.
2. **The B switch is a dead position.** The question it existed to answer — what a state button
   is made of — was closed by law rather than by pixels (buttons are digital, hosted in glass),
   so its `steel` and `hybrid` positions no longer have a target. The switch is still in the
   harness pending removal (#203 closing #191). Don't report the dead positions.
3. **Head and row boards do not share one column** (#196) — see §4.3. Deferred to a wider card.
4. **Width thresholds** at which things drop are provisional and untested (#162).
5. **The row state element's sizing formula** (`7.15px/char`) is flagged for re-measurement
   (#155). Report only if a specific size actively costs Merle a tap or a read.
6. **The group set shown is a simplified demo subset** of the real staff/mechanic list a later
   tier will wire (#159) — report only if the *demo* subset itself misleads Merle.
7. **The floating pins are hidden pending a cleverer home** (#204, §4.5). Their rehoming is
   a known open design task. Don't report "the counts are missing" as news — but **do** report
   if Merle actually needs a count somewhere the head board doesn't already give him one.
8. **A two-deck head** (slot rack in its own recessed tray, above the board+name row) is a
   parked structural idea from Jac's mockup, not yet ruled. The single-row head you see is
   current.
9. **Pixel fidelity to Jac's mockup is a known gap.** A port of three ring-construction recipes
   was attempted and reverted the same night for regressions. Don't audit the card against any
   mockup — you don't have one, and Prompt A's §0 rule applies: judge the bar, not the intent.

## 7 · What we most want to know

If the run produces nothing else, answer these:

1. Standing in the yard with one glove off, **can Merle tell what is on fire in two seconds** —
   and does anything non-urgent steal that glance?
2. **Does he know what to do next**, or only what the state currently is? This card deliberately
   chose *condition* over *verb* for its one row-level control (#182) — an explicit bet that
   knowing "3D OVERDUE" serves him better than being told "Return". **Test that bet honestly.**
3. **The boards are now both the readout and the button.** State at rest, action verb on hover,
   press to act; the separate status chip is gone. This is the newest and least-tested idea in
   the card. Does one element doing both jobs read clearly to Merle, or does he miss that the
   thing he's reading is also the thing he can press? Note especially that **the hover verb is
   invisible on a touch device** — say plainly whether the design survives without hover.
4. **The group/row split — a head that MOVES vs. a row that LIGHTS UP.** Does that read as two
   different kinds of thing, or just as inconsistency?
5. **Search lives inside a group's head board.** Is that discoverable at all? Merle will not
   hunt. If he'd never find it, say so bluntly.
6. Nine groups is a lot. **Does the grouping help him, or does he lose the two rows that matter
   inside seven groups that don't?**
7. Every count pin was hidden this week (#204). **Does anything now feel uncounted** — does he
   have to open a group to learn something he should have seen closed?
8. What does he learn here that **somebody else needs to know**, with no way to pass it on?
